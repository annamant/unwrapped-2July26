Once called the Oxford Street of South London — Rye Lane, Peckham.

It runs south from Peckham Rye station through Southwark SE15: a Victorian shopping street you walk, not a mall. Buses, produce crates, shopfronts stacked tight from the railway bridge toward Nunhead.

After the station opened in 1865 the lane grew into a major high street — home to grand stores like Jones & Higgins and a “golden mile” of retail. Today it still thrives as everyday high street: butchers, fishmongers, tropical produce, fabric shops, cafés, indoor markets — and Peckhamplex, the independent cinema on the lane.

Did you know? Behind the shopfronts sits Copeland Park and the Bussey Building — Victorian industrial warehouses named for George Gibson Bussey, whose firm made cricket bats at Museum Works off Rye Lane. The site is now creative studios and cultural space.
